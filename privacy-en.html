Memo Clock Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 27, 2026

Memo Clock (“the app”) is for recording and reviewing study cards. The app has no accounts of its own, the developer runs no servers of their own, and the app contains no analytics, advertising or tracking tools and no in-app purchases. What you record is stored on your device; if your device is signed in to iCloud, it is also stored in the private iCloud space of your own Apple ID and synced between your devices, and the developer cannot see or obtain it. The one exception is the Market: what you choose to upload to it and the reports you send are stored in the app’s public iCloud database, where the developer can see them; the developer has each upload looked over first by Anthropic’s AI, Claude, and once an upload is approved anyone can download it (see section 7). Apart from iCloud, the app goes online by itself in one place only: once you tap Download, it downloads the handwritten-formula reader from GitHub (see section 3).

1. What the app stores

To make its features work, the app stores:

  • Cards: title, prompt, hints, answer, explanation, the name of the deck it is in, type and favorite status; handwriting strokes and handwriting images; photos; what is covered (words picked in the answer, or boxes drawn on a photo or on handwriting); where a card came from (a notebook page or a logbook cell); how it is reviewed (a quick review for a few days, or on the memory curve) and whether it was taken out of review; the text recognized in handwriting and photos; and the card’s state on the memory curve: its mastery, when it was last answered, how today’s answers went, the review days a Know By planned for it, and the day it was set to Not Again Today.
  • Review history: when each answer was given and how long it took, the grade you chose (Forgot, Fuzzy, Got it; an Easy chosen in an earlier version is kept as it was), the card’s mastery before and after the answer, the answer’s moment on the memory curve, how many cards came between it and the card’s last appearance, and the next review time worked out from it. Self-test records from earlier versions are kept as they were; self-testing has been removed.
  • The daily review plan: the most reviews a day; the batch of cards set for each study day (a study day starts at 4 a.m.) and the cards moved to the next day; the days you checked in and whether a make-up was used; which decks and books are paused; and each Know By you set up: its name, the date to know it by, the decks and books it covers (or the cards you picked) and the cards you took out of it. At the start of each study day, the app plans the cards of each Know By again, as a whole. A review intensity chosen in an earlier version is still kept, but no longer used.
  • Notebooks and files: each notebook’s name, cover, paper style, paper size and paper color; each page’s name and the strokes on it; imported PDF, Word and PowerPoint pages and web pages; photos taken or chosen into a notebook; the parts of pages boxed for review; and the text recognized on the pages.
  • Logbooks: each logbook’s name, paper color and whether its lines are shown; each page’s name and place; in each cell, what is written on the left and the content on the right (body, example, aside, red text, formulas, photos and links); the style of its text (size, pen color, highlighter, centering); where each photo sits in its cell and how big it is; what you write on photos with Apple Pencil; when each cell was written; what is covered in the logbook; and which cells you are learning.
  • Notes: title, text and the photos taped to them; a note jotted during review in an earlier version still remembers the card it belongs to.
  • Word books: which books are on your Desk; the name, cover and words of each word book you made, and how many words a day each book gives you; each word’s state on the memory curve in each book and when it comes back; the notes you write on words; and, for each answer (in a Test too), when you gave it, the grade you chose and the word’s mastery before and after.
  • Widgets: which widgets you placed on the Today page, in Review and beside your books, their order, size and place, and what you entered or set in them, such as countdowns and the photos you chose for them, the timetable (class names, teachers, colors, notes, class times and places, the decks or books linked to a class, and the time blocks you add: name, place, weekday, time and which week), which cards the Review widget beside a book goes through (that book’s course, or all of them) and how soon it moves on to the next card, checklists, habit check-ins, counters, links, sticky-note text and handwriting, photos you chose, the lengths of pomodoros and other timers, and the number of pomodoros finished each day. The events and to-dos in Schedule are not kept by the app (see section 4).
  • Focus history: the start and end of each stretch of focus (reviewing, studying words or taking notes in the app, a pomodoro or other timer running, or a class) and what it counts toward (a deck, a notebook, a logbook, a word book, a class or a category you made), kept for about the last 400 days; and the names of the categories you made and what is linked to them.
  • Settings: review settings, reminder times, sound and haptics, language, the language for Say it, Desk size, page drag sensitivity, the timetable’s before-class and after-class reminders and its class focus switch, Auto Backup and the other settings.
  • Desk layout: whether the Desk is set to Line Up or Free, the order of things when lined up, where each item lies when arranged freely, which things are stacked into a pile, and which things are put away in Archive.
  • Recently Deleted: the cards, decks, notebooks and pages, logbooks and pages, cells, notes, word books and words, focus categories, classes and time blocks, and Know Bys you delete, with what they hold, for 30 days.
  • Inbox: text, links and images you send to the app from the system Share menu and have not yet put into a book.
  • Market: the username you last uploaded with, the school, major and year you gave when you first opened the Market (used to filter it and to fill in your uploads), and which authors you blocked. What you upload to the Market and the reports you send are described in section 7.
  • Other app state: unfinished drafts, today’s review progress (which cards have come up today and how many came between them), actions you can still undo (including undoing a restore), which cards were imported recently, the book you last put something into, how far you have marked a word book’s Test, whether you have seen the logbook guide, the last day the memory curve was moved on to, and the counts used to decide when to ask you to rate the app on the App Store (time used, number of sessions and when you were last asked).

2. Where it is kept

  • Your device: everything above is stored in the app’s storage on your device.
  • iCloud sync: if your device is signed in to iCloud, cards (with their memory-curve state), review history, the daily review plan and check-ins, Know Bys, notebooks and files, logbooks (with text styles, photo positions and what is written on photos), notes, word books, the focus categories you made, Recently Deleted, the Desk’s piles and Archive, and widgets (including photos in widgets, the timetable and its time blocks) sync automatically through Apple’s iCloud to your other devices signed in to the same Apple ID.
  • Study data in iCloud: when you tap “Upload to iCloud” in Settings › iCloud Sync, or after you complete your reviews for the day, the app saves a copy of your study data in your private iCloud space. It contains cards, review history, notebooks and files, logbooks, notes, widgets, the Desk’s piles and Archive, which decks and books are paused, plus check-ins, focus history, settings, deletion records, the upload time and the type of device it came from (for example, iPad); word books, focus categories, Recently Deleted and each day’s batch of cards are not in it and sync only as described above. It is read when you tap “Sync to This Device” on another device.
  • Backup files: when you tap Export Backup in Settings › iCloud Sync › Backup & Restore, the app writes everything (cards, review history, the daily review plan and check-ins, notebooks and files, logbooks, notes, word books, focus categories and focus history, widgets, Recently Deleted, the Desk layout and settings) into one .memoclockbackup file and hands it to the system Share menu, so you decide where it is kept and who receives it. Auto Backup (once a week unless you choose every day or Off) writes the same file into the Memo Clock folder in your iCloud Drive, which you can see in the Files app; when your device is not signed in to iCloud it writes it on this device (the Memo Clock folder under On My iPad or On My iPhone in Files), or into a folder you choose. Auto Backup keeps only the latest 4 files it wrote, and writes none while the app holds nothing yet. A backup file is not encrypted apart from that: whoever has the file can read what is in it. Restore from Backup reads only the file you choose.
  • This device only: inbox items not yet put into a book, drafts, today’s review progress and undo records, the marks of a word book Test in progress, whether you have seen the logbook guide, the last day the memory curve was moved on to, the rating-prompt counts, the timetable’s reminder and class focus switches, and your Market username, school, major, year and blocked authors are never synced or stored in iCloud or in backup files. The Desk layout is not synced to your other devices and is not in the study data in iCloud; only backup files have it.
  • System widgets: so that Memo Clock widgets on the Home Screen and Lock Screen, its controls in Control Center and Memo Clock in the system Share menu have something to show, the app writes a summary on your device: how many cards are due on each of the next few days and about how long they will take, your check-in streak, how many you reviewed today, the title, prompt, answer and deck of the next 6 cards due, how many cards each deck holds and how many are due, the names of your notebooks and logbooks and of their last few pages, the cover and page count of your notebooks (and, for up to 24 most recently written ones, the top of the page last written on), the content of up to 40 cells of each logbook (with its text styles) and small copies of their photos (the cells you are learning, the last cells of the last page and those written today), the book you last put something into, the title and first lines of your notes (and, for up to 24 recent ones, a small copy of the first photo), your countdowns, today’s focus minutes and goal, and the number and status of pomodoros. The summary stays on this device and only the app, its widgets and its share extension can read it. Widgets on the Lock Screen can be seen while the device is locked. The line on an A Line widget is the one you write in its Edit Widget settings; the system keeps it with the widget’s settings, and the app keeps no copy.
  • Recitation Lock: when Recitation Lock is on, a Live Activity on the Lock Screen shows the title of the locked card and the time left. It is created on your device.
  • Timers: while a pomodoro, countdown or stopwatch runs, a Live Activity on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island shows what it counts toward (or the timer’s name when it counts toward nothing), a line about the current stretch and the time. It is created on your device.
  • Device backups: depending on your system settings, iCloud Backup or a computer backup may also include the app’s data (except the handwritten-formula reader).
  • The Market: what you upload to the Market and the reports you send are not in your own iCloud; they are stored in the app’s public iCloud database, which belongs to the developer’s iCloud container (see section 7).

Apart from the syncing and storage through Apple’s iCloud described above, the files you export or share yourself, and what you upload to the Market and the reports you send (see section 7), the app does not send your content anywhere; downloading the handwritten-formula reader only fetches files and uploads nothing; a web page you imported may connect to the internet by itself when you open it (see “Web pages” in section 3). The developer does not sell or rent your content, and gives it to no third party, except that Market uploads are given to Anthropic for their first review (see section 7). iCloud is provided by Apple and is governed by your Apple ID settings and Apple’s Privacy Policy.

3. Processing done on your device

  • Text recognition: Chinese and English in handwriting, photos and imported pages are read on your device by Apple’s built-in text recognition, so you can find them with search in the app and pick words after taking a photo or scanning. The recognized text is stored with the cards and notebooks and syncs through iCloud. A web page’s words are read from the page’s file, likewise only for search.
  • Formulas: formulas are typeset on your device by a component built into the app (KaTeX); no network is needed.
  • Handwritten formulas: these need iOS 18 or later. The reader they use (about 185 MB) is not installed with the app; it is downloaded from GitHub (github.com/wojiaozyh123-hub/memoclock-formula-reader) only when you tap Write in the formula keys and then Download, or tap Download in Settings › New Card › Handwritten Formulas. The download only fetches files and uploads none of your content; GitHub receives your network address and the other details a browser’s visit gives it, and is governed by its own privacy policy. Every file is checked against its size and SHA-256 when it arrives, and is not used if it does not match. The reader is kept on this device only: it does not sync through iCloud, is not in the study data or backup files, and is left out of device backups; in Settings › New Card › Handwritten Formulas you can stop the download or delete it. The formulas you write by hand and the formulas you frame in a photo are read by it on your device and are sent nowhere.
  • Ambient sounds: rain, waves and the other sounds are synthesized live on your device; no audio is downloaded. While one plays, Control Center and the Lock Screen show the name of the sound. They pause while you use Say it, and play again afterwards.
  • Say it: when you hold to speak in a logbook, or start Say it from its Home Screen widget, Control Center, the Lock Screen or the Action button, your words are turned into text on your device by Apple’s built-in speech recognition and written into a logbook cell; it does not go over the network (the first time a language is used, the system may need to download its recognition files). The sound of your voice is not kept or uploaded.
  • Say Your Timetable: in the timetable, hold to speak, or type, when your classes are. What you say is turned into text on your device by the same speech recognition as Say it: from iOS 26, the system’s own dictation model, which runs on the device; on earlier systems recognition is set to happen on the device only, and where the device has no recognition files for the language it does not recognize at all rather than go over the network. The first time a language is used, the system may need to download its recognition files. The text is then read into classes by Apple’s own on-device language model where it can be used (Apple Intelligence on, and the model reading the app’s language); where it cannot, or where it finds no class, rules built into the app read it instead. All of this happens on your device: nothing of it goes over the network or reaches the developer. The classes and time blocks found are first drawn on your timetable for you to look at, and are saved only when you tap Add to Timetable; neither the sound of your voice nor the sentence itself is kept.
  • Word books: the lists of the six word books that come with the app are installed with it, nothing is downloaded; words and examples are read aloud on your device by the system’s own voices.
  • Anki import: the .apkg file you choose is read on your device, and its text, images and review history (including how long each answer took) become cards and review history; the file itself is not kept or uploaded.
  • Opening a shared file: a book or a deck someone shares with you (a Memo Clock file) is read on your device and shown to you first; it is saved only when you tap “Put on my desk”.
  • File import: each page of a PDF you choose, or open in Memo Clock from another app, is saved into the matching notebook and syncs through iCloud. Word and PowerPoint files are laid out on your device by the system’s own web view and turned into PDF pages, which are saved into a notebook the same way; the original file is not kept. A web page file (.html, .htm) is saved as it is as a page of a notebook and syncs through iCloud.
  • Web pages: when you open a web page in a notebook, the app shows it as a browser would. Scripts, images, fonts and other things the page links to on the internet are loaded from the sites they are on, and the page’s own scripts may connect to the internet as well; those sites receive your network address and the other details a browser’s visit gives them, and are governed by their own privacy policies. The app does not block these requests; it gives the page only its own file and hands none of your other content to it. Website data the page stores (such as cookies) stays on this device. A link you open in the page is opened in the system browser.
  • Taking photos and scanning: the text in the photo you take, scan or choose is read on your device; the words you pick and the parts you frame go into a card or a logbook cell, and the whole photo is not kept unless you add it to a notebook.
  • Exporting and sharing: only when you tap it does the app make a file, which it hands to the system Share menu so you decide who receives it and where it is kept. A deck can be shared as a PDF, a spreadsheet (CSV), an Anki deck or a Memo Clock file; a notebook or a logbook as a Memo Clock file; cards you select as a share file; and a notebook page can be exported as a PDF or an image, a whole notebook as a PDF. The file holds those cards or that book, with their photos and handwriting, text styles and what is written on photos, and no review history or memory-curve state. An upload to the Market is a Memo Clock file of this kind (see section 7).
  • Links in widgets: opened in the system browser only when you tap them.

4. System permissions

  • Camera: requested only when you choose to take a photo or scan. The photos and scanned pages go into your cards, notebooks, logbooks or notes.
  • Photos: picking from your library uses the system photo picker; the app receives only the photos you select and cannot see the rest of your library. The photos you pick go into cards, notebooks, logbooks, notes, photo widgets or countdowns.
  • Notifications: requested when you turn on Notifications, when you tap OK on “What time each day should we remind you to review?” after you first finish the day’s reviews, when you first turn on a timetable’s Before Class or After Class reminder, or when you start a timer for the first time. All of these are scheduled on your device, and the app has no push server of its own: review reminders contain no card content; a class reminder gives the class name, its time and place, and an after-class reminder how many of its cards are due; timer alerts give only what the timer counts toward or the name of its section, and its length.
  • App icon badge: when “Show Due Count on App Icon” is on, the icon shows how many cards are due today.
  • Microphone: requested the first time you use Say it or Say Your Timetable; it is on only while you speak, and off as soon as you finish.
  • Speech recognition: requested the first time you use Say it or Say Your Timetable where the system’s speech recognition is needed, to turn what you say into text on your device.
  • Calendars: requested only when you tap “Connect Calendar & Reminders” in the Schedule widget. Schedule reads and writes your calendars directly: it shows your events, and the events you add, change or delete here are saved in your calendars, which send their alerts; the app keeps no copy and uploads nothing, and only remembers on this device which calendars you chose to hide.
  • Reminders: requested together with Calendars when you tap “Connect Calendar & Reminders”, or when you tap Connect by the to-dos in Schedule. To-dos live in your reminders: Schedule shows them, and the to-dos you add, change, tick off or delete here are saved in Reminders, which sends their alerts; the app keeps no copy and uploads nothing, and only remembers on this device which lists you chose to hide.
  • iCloud and the Market: the Market needs no other system permission. Browsing and downloading need no sign-in; uploading and reporting need your device to be signed in to iCloud, and the app then gets only an anonymous number iCloud gives it for you, never your Apple ID, name or email address.
  • The app does not use location, contacts or health data, and does not read other files on your device except what you hand it yourself: files you choose in the file picker (a PDF, a Word or PowerPoint file, a web page, an Anki deck, a share file or a backup file), what you send to it from another app with Share or Open in Memo Clock, and the folder you choose for Auto Backup.

5. What the app does not do

  • It has no accounts of its own and collects no identity information such as your name, phone number or email address, except in feedback emails you choose to send (see section 10). An upload to the Market carries the username you made up and the anonymous number iCloud gives the app for you; a report carries that number (see section 7).
  • It contains no analytics, advertising or tracking tools and does not track you across apps.
  • Apart from syncing and storage through your own iCloud, the files you export or share yourself, and what you upload to the Market and the reports you send, it does not send your content or the recognized text anywhere; downloading the handwritten-formula reader only fetches files and uploads nothing; for a web page you imported connecting by itself when you open it, see section 3.
  • The app itself connects to no AI service: Say Your Timetable uses Apple’s own model on your device, and the handwritten-formula reader is downloaded to your device and runs on it. The only outside AI is the first review of Market uploads: the review tool on the developer’s computer gives Anthropic what you upload to the Market, and nothing else of yours (see section 7).
  • It has no in-app purchases or subscriptions. Payment and refunds are handled by Apple through the App Store; the developer never receives your payment details.

6. Deleting your data

  • The cards, decks, notebooks and pages, logbooks and pages, cells, notes, word books and words, focus categories, classes and time blocks, and Know Bys you delete in the app go into Recently Deleted (under ··· at the top of the Desk) for 30 days: until then you can put them back, or tap Delete Now; after 30 days they are deleted. Deleting a card also deletes its review history and images; deleting a deck deletes its cards and their review history; deleting a notebook deletes all its pages; deleting a logbook deletes all its pages, cells and photos and the cards made from it. Deleting a Know By deletes only its date and which cards it holds; the cards themselves stay. Deletions and Recently Deleted sync to your other devices signed in to the same Apple ID.
  • Deleting a widget deletes what it holds at once.
  • Deleting a word book you made deletes its list of words; what you learned in it and your answers in it are not deleted with it. A word book that comes with the app is only taken off the Desk, and what you learned in it stays.
  • The handwritten-formula reader: delete it in Settings › New Card › Handwritten Formulas.
  • Backup files: a backup you exported is deleted by you, wherever you kept it; the files Auto Backup writes are in iCloud Drive › Memo Clock in the Files app (or the folder you chose), and can be deleted there.
  • The Market: once an upload is sent, the app has no button to withdraw or delete it; to have it withdrawn, taken down or deleted, write to the developer, who does so and deletes it from the public database if the request is reasonable (see section 7).
  • Deleting the app deletes what it stored on that device. Data synced to iCloud, the study data in iCloud and the automatic backups in iCloud Drive are not deleted with it; to delete them, open Settings on your device, tap your name, go to iCloud storage management, find Memo Clock and delete its data; the automatic backups can also be deleted in the Files app. Your Market uploads, their listed copies and your reports are not in your iCloud storage: neither deleting the app nor deleting Memo Clock’s data in iCloud removes them.
  • Data in device backups is managed by your backup settings.

7. The Market

The Market is where students share decks and books with each other, for free.

  • What is uploaded: in Upload to Market you choose a deck, a notebook or a logbook, and can pick only some of its cards or pages. What goes up is a Memo Clock file, the same as Share this book makes: the cards’ text, pictures, handwriting and covered parts; each notebook page’s strokes, the PDF page or photo under it and the text recognized on it; the content of each logbook cell (with its text styles, its photos and what is written on them); the words of anything outside the book that it links to; and when each of these was written and last changed. It holds no review history and no memory-curve state. With the file go pictures of its first pages (the first one as the cover); the name, course, school, major and year you enter (school, major and year are optional); your username and description (up to 1,000 characters); the first card’s prompt or the first page’s name; the numbers of cards and pages; and your copyright promise: each upload asks you to tick that the content is your own original work or that you own its copyright, and the promise’s version and the time you ticked it are kept with the upload.
  • Who you are to it: uploading and reporting need your device to be signed in to iCloud. CloudKit notes on each record who made it: an anonymous number iCloud gives the app for you, which is not your Apple ID, name or email address and from which the developer cannot find them out. Everything one person uploads has the same number; once an upload is approved, the number is made public with your username on its listed copy, and Block author works by it.
  • Where it is kept: in the app’s public iCloud database. It is on Apple’s servers and belongs to the developer’s iCloud container; it is not in your private iCloud space and does not use your iCloud storage.
  • Who can see it: until it is reviewed, an upload can be read only by you and the developer. Once it is approved, the developer puts up a copy of it as a listing, which is not changed after that; every user can see it in the Market and download it, even without signing in to iCloud. The listing carries the file and details above, your username and anonymous number, and when it was approved. The review’s answer (approved or not, and a line from the developer to you) is also kept in the public database, readable by anyone, and the app shows it only to you, in My uploads. The developer can see every upload and every report.
  • Review: a review tool on the developer’s computer regularly fetches each upload not yet reviewed to that computer, and first gives it to Anthropic’s AI, Claude, through Anthropic’s API, to look for inappropriate content, signs of copied material and wrong answers. What goes to Anthropic is the upload’s text (the cards’ titles, prompts, answers and explanations, the words and asides in each logbook cell, each notebook page’s name, the text recognized in handwriting and photos, and the words of anything outside the book it links to), its pictures (the cards’ pictures, photos in a logbook, the PDF page or photo under a notebook page, and the pictures of the first pages, made no longer than 1,600 pixels), and the name, course, school, major, year, username, description and numbers of cards and pages; the anonymous number is not sent. Anthropic is an independent third party and handles what it receives under its own terms and privacy policy. The AI’s findings are only a suggestion to the developer: every upload is looked at by the developer in person, who then approves it or declines it, and when declining writes you a line saying why. The uploaded file and the AI’s findings stay on the developer’s computer.
  • After you upload: an upload cannot be changed once it is sent, and the app has no button to withdraw or delete it; to change something, upload it again, and it is reviewed again. To withdraw an upload still in review, take a listing down or have them deleted, write to 3528872473@qq.com. The developer checks each request and, if it is reasonable (for example, the upload is confirmed to be yours, or it infringes someone else’s rights), does as you ask and deletes it from the public database. When the developer takes a listing down, it no longer shows in the Market, but its record stays in the public database until the developer deletes it; your upload itself also stays in the public database after it is reviewed (readable only by you and the developer). Copies others have already put on their desks stay with them, and the developer cannot delete those. Neither deleting the app nor deleting Memo Clock’s data in your device’s iCloud settings removes anything from the Market.
  • Reporting and blocking: when looking at someone else’s upload you can report it, choosing a reason: inappropriate content, copyright infringement or incorrect content. A report is kept in the public database with which listing it is about, the reason you chose and your anonymous number, and can be read only by you and the developer; after looking at it, the developer can take the listing down. Reporting also needs iCloud. Block author is kept on this device only and sent to no one: what that person uploaded is no longer shown on this device, and you can undo it.
  • Contacting the developer: the Market has no contact button of its own; to write to the developer, use Settings › About › Contact Support (see section 10).
  • What the Market does not have: no comments, and no friends, follows or messages. Browsing and downloading only read from the public database; the app writes down nothing of what you looked at or downloaded, and tells the author nothing.
  • Downloading someone else’s: when you open a listing, the app downloads its file from the public database into a temporary folder on this device and reads it there to show it to you. Only when you tap Import all, or import the cards or pages you pick, is it put on your desk; it then becomes your own deck or book, kept, synced and backed up like what you record yourself. It brings none of the author’s review history: you start learning it from the beginning. An imported notebook or logbook also remembers which book it came from, so the app knows you already have it. What you do not import is not kept with your content.

8. Children

The app collects no identity information such as names, phone numbers or email addresses from anyone, children included. Your Market username is one you make up and is shown publicly with what you upload: do not use your real name or contact details in it.

9. Changes to this policy

If a future version changes how data is handled, we will update this policy first and update the date both in the app and on the web page.

10. Feedback and contact

Settings › About › Contact Support opens a message to the developer, addressed to 3528872473@qq.com, in your device’s mail app. The message comes with the app version, device model, system version and interface language filled in, and you can change or delete any of it before sending. The developer receives the message only if you send it yourself; it then includes your email address, what you wrote and anything you attached. This is used only to reply to you and to improve the app, never for anything else, and is not shared with any third party. When the device has no app that can send email, the address is shown so you can copy it and write yourself.

Questions about this policy or how data is handled, and requests to withdraw, take down or delete what you put in the Market, can also be sent to 3528872473@qq.com.
