Memo Clock Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 22, 2026

Memo Clock (“the app”) is for recording and reviewing study cards. The app has no accounts, the developer runs no servers, and the app contains no analytics, advertising or tracking tools and no in-app purchases. What you record is stored on your device; if your device is signed in to iCloud, it is also stored in the private iCloud space of your own Apple ID and synced between your devices. The developer cannot see or obtain any of it. Apart from iCloud, the app goes online by itself in one place only: once you tap Download, it downloads the handwritten-formula reader from GitHub (see section 3).

1. What the app stores

To make its features work, the app stores:

  • Cards: title, prompt, hints, answer, explanation, the name of the deck it is in, type and favorite status; handwriting strokes and handwriting images; photos; what is covered (words picked in the answer, or boxes drawn on a photo or on handwriting); where a card came from (a notebook page or a logbook cell); how it is reviewed (a quick review for a few days, or on the memory curve) and whether it was taken out of review; and the text recognized in handwriting and photos.
  • Review history: when each review happened and how long it took, the grade you chose (Forgot, Fuzzy, Got it; an Easy chosen in an earlier version is kept as it was) and whether it was a self-test, plus the next review time and memory-model state worked out from it.
  • The daily review plan: the most reviews a day and the review intensity, the batch of cards set for each study day and the cards moved to later days, the days you checked in and whether a make-up was used, and which decks and books are paused.
  • Notebooks and files: each notebook’s name, cover, paper size and paper color; each page’s name and the strokes on it; imported PDF, Word and PowerPoint pages and web pages; photos taken or chosen into a notebook; the parts of pages boxed for review; and the text recognized on the pages.
  • Logbooks: each logbook’s name and paper color; each page’s name and place; in each cell, what is written on the left and the content on the right (body, example, aside, red text, formulas, photos and links), and when the cell was written; what is covered in the logbook; and which cells you are learning.
  • Notes: title, text and the photos taped to them; a note jotted during review in an earlier version still remembers the card it belongs to.
  • Word books: which books are on your Desk; the name, cover and words of each word book you made, and how many words a day each book gives you; each word’s memory-model state in each book and when it comes back; the notes you write on words; and when you answered each word (in a Test too) and the grade you chose.
  • Widgets: which widgets you placed on the Today page, in Review and beside your books, their order, size and place, and what you entered in them, such as countdowns and the photos you chose for them, timetables (class names, class times and places, the decks or books linked to a class, and the reminder and focus switches), checklists, habit check-ins, counters, links, sticky-note text and handwriting, photos you chose, the lengths of pomodoros and other timers, and the number of pomodoros finished each day. The events and to-dos in Schedule are not kept by the app (see section 4).
  • Focus history: the start and end of each stretch of focus (reviewing, studying words or taking notes in the app, a pomodoro or other timer running, or a class) and what it counts toward (a deck, a notebook, a logbook, a word book, a class or a category you made), kept for about the last 400 days; and the names of the categories you made and what is linked to them.
  • Settings: review settings, reminder times, sound and haptics, language, the language for Say it, Desk size, Auto Backup and the other settings.
  • Desk layout: whether the Desk is set to Line Up or Free, the order of things when lined up, where each item lies when arranged freely, which things are stacked into a pile, and which things are put away in Archive.
  • Recently Deleted: the cards, decks, notebooks and pages, logbooks and pages, cells, notes, word books and words, focus categories and classes you delete, with what they hold, for 30 days.
  • Inbox: text, links and images you send to the app from the system Share menu and have not yet put into a book.
  • Other app state: unfinished drafts, progress through an unfinished review round, actions you can still undo (including undoing a restore), which cards were imported recently, the book you last put something into, how far you have marked a word book’s Test, and the counts used to decide when to ask you to rate the app on the App Store (time used, number of sessions and when you were last asked).

2. Where it is kept

  • Your device: everything above is stored in the app’s storage on your device.
  • iCloud sync: if your device is signed in to iCloud, cards, review history, the daily review plan and check-ins, notebooks and files, logbooks, notes, word books, the focus categories you made, Recently Deleted, the Desk’s piles and Archive, and widgets (including photos in widgets) sync automatically through Apple’s iCloud to your other devices signed in to the same Apple ID.
  • Study data in iCloud: when you tap “Upload to iCloud” in Settings › iCloud Sync, or after you complete your reviews for the day, the app saves a copy of your study data in your private iCloud space. It contains cards, review history, notebooks and files, logbooks, notes, widgets, the Desk’s piles and Archive, which decks and books are paused, plus check-ins, focus history, settings, deletion records, the upload time and the type of device it came from (for example, iPad); word books, focus categories, Recently Deleted and each day’s batch of cards are not in it and sync only as described above. It is read when you tap “Sync to This Device” on another device.
  • Backup files: when you tap Export Backup in Settings › iCloud Sync › Backup & Restore, the app writes everything (cards, review history, the daily review plan and check-ins, notebooks and files, logbooks, notes, word books, focus categories and focus history, widgets, Recently Deleted, the Desk layout and settings) into one .memoclockbackup file and hands it to the system Share menu, so you decide where it is kept and who receives it. Auto Backup (once a week unless you choose every day or Off) writes the same file into the Memo Clock folder in your iCloud Drive, which you can see in the Files app; when your device is not signed in to iCloud it writes it on this device (the Memo Clock folder under On My iPad or On My iPhone in Files), or into a folder you choose. Auto Backup keeps only the latest 4 files it wrote, and writes none while the app holds nothing yet. A backup file is not encrypted apart from that: whoever has the file can read what is in it. Restore from Backup reads only the file you choose.
  • This device only: inbox items not yet put into a book, drafts, review progress and undo records, the marks of a word book Test in progress, and the rating-prompt counts are never synced or stored in iCloud or in backup files. The Desk layout is not synced to your other devices and is not in the study data in iCloud; only backup files have it.
  • System widgets: so that Memo Clock widgets on the Home Screen and Lock Screen, its controls in Control Center and Memo Clock in the system Share menu have something to show, the app writes a summary on your device: how many cards are due on each of the next few days and about how long they will take, your check-in streak, how many you reviewed today, the title, prompt, answer and deck of the next 6 cards due, how many cards each deck holds and how many are due, the names of your notebooks and logbooks and of their last few pages, the cover and page count of your notebooks (and, for up to 24 most recently written ones, the top of the page last written on), the content of up to 40 cells of each logbook with small copies of their photos (the cells you are learning, the last cells of the last page and those written today), the book you last put something into, the title and first lines of your notes (and, for up to 24 recent ones, a small copy of the first photo), your countdowns, today’s focus minutes and goal, and the number and status of pomodoros. The summary stays on this device and only the app, its widgets and its share extension can read it. Widgets on the Lock Screen can be seen while the device is locked. The line on an A Line widget is the one you write in its Edit Widget settings; the system keeps it with the widget’s settings, and the app keeps no copy.
  • Recitation Lock: when Recitation Lock is on, a Live Activity on the Lock Screen shows the title of the locked card and the time left. It is created on your device.
  • Timers: while a pomodoro, countdown or stopwatch runs, a Live Activity on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island shows what it counts toward (or the timer’s name when it counts toward nothing), a line about the current stretch and the time. It is created on your device.
  • Device backups: depending on your system settings, iCloud Backup or a computer backup may also include the app’s data (except the handwritten-formula reader).

Apart from the syncing and storage through Apple’s iCloud described above and the files you export or share yourself, the app does not send your content anywhere; downloading the handwritten-formula reader only fetches files and uploads nothing; a web page you imported may connect to the internet by itself when you open it (see “Web pages” in section 3). The developer does not sell, rent or share your content with anyone. iCloud is provided by Apple and is governed by your Apple ID settings and Apple’s Privacy Policy.

3. Processing done on your device

  • Text recognition: Chinese and English in handwriting, photos and imported pages are read on your device by Apple’s built-in text recognition, so you can find them with search in the app and pick words after taking a photo or scanning. The recognized text is stored with the cards and notebooks and syncs through iCloud. A web page’s words are read from the page’s file, likewise only for search.
  • Formulas: formulas are typeset on your device by a component built into the app (KaTeX); no network is needed.
  • Handwritten formulas: these need iOS 18 or later. The reader they use (about 185 MB) is not installed with the app; it is downloaded from GitHub (github.com/wojiaozyh123-hub/memoclock-formula-reader) only when you tap Write in the formula keys and then Download, or tap Download in Settings › New Card › Handwritten Formulas. The download only fetches files and uploads none of your content; GitHub receives your network address and the other details a browser’s visit gives it, and is governed by its own privacy policy. Every file is checked against its size and SHA-256 when it arrives, and is not used if it does not match. The reader is kept on this device only: it does not sync through iCloud, is not in the study data or backup files, and is left out of device backups; in Settings › New Card › Handwritten Formulas you can stop the download or delete it. The formulas you write by hand and the formulas you frame in a photo are read by it on your device and are sent nowhere.
  • Ambient sounds: rain, waves and the other sounds are synthesized live on your device; no audio is downloaded. While one plays, Control Center and the Lock Screen show the name of the sound. They pause while you use Say it, and play again afterwards.
  • Say it: when you hold to speak in a logbook, or start Say it from its Home Screen widget, Control Center, the Lock Screen or the Action button, your words are turned into text on your device by Apple’s built-in speech recognition and written into a logbook cell; it does not go over the network (the first time a language is used, the system may need to download its recognition files). The sound of your voice is not kept or uploaded.
  • Word books: the lists of the six word books that come with the app are installed with it, nothing is downloaded; words and examples are read aloud on your device by the system’s own voices.
  • Anki import: the .apkg file you choose is read on your device, and its text, images and review history (including how long each answer took) become cards and review history; the file itself is not kept or uploaded.
  • Opening a shared file: a book or a deck someone shares with you (a Memo Clock file) is read on your device and shown to you first; it is saved only when you tap “Put on my desk”.
  • File import: each page of a PDF you choose, or open in Memo Clock from another app, is saved into the matching notebook and syncs through iCloud. Word and PowerPoint files are laid out on your device by the system’s own web view and turned into PDF pages, which are saved into a notebook the same way; the original file is not kept. A web page file (.html, .htm) is saved as it is as a page of a notebook and syncs through iCloud.
  • Web pages: when you open a web page in a notebook, the app shows it as a browser would. Scripts, images, fonts and other things the page links to on the internet are loaded from the sites they are on, and the page’s own scripts may connect to the internet as well; those sites receive your network address and the other details a browser’s visit gives them, and are governed by their own privacy policies. The app does not block these requests; it gives the page only its own file and hands none of your other content to it. Website data the page stores (such as cookies) stays on this device. A link you open in the page is opened in the system browser.
  • Taking photos and scanning: the text in the photo you take, scan or choose is read on your device; the words you pick and the parts you frame go into a card or a logbook cell, and the whole photo is not kept unless you add it to a notebook.
  • Exporting and sharing: only when you tap it does the app make a file, which it hands to the system Share menu so you decide who receives it and where it is kept. A deck can be shared as a PDF, a spreadsheet (CSV), an Anki deck or a Memo Clock file; a notebook or a logbook as a Memo Clock file; cards you select as a share file; and a notebook page can be exported as a PDF or an image, a whole notebook as a PDF. The file holds those cards or that book, with their photos and handwriting, and no review history.
  • Links in widgets: opened in the system browser only when you tap them.

4. System permissions

  • Camera: requested only when you choose to take a photo or scan. The photos and scanned pages go into your cards, notebooks, logbooks or notes.
  • Photos: picking from your library uses the system photo picker; the app receives only the photos you select and cannot see the rest of your library. The photos you pick go into cards, notebooks, logbooks, notes, photo widgets or countdowns.
  • Notifications: requested when you turn on Notifications, when you tap OK on “What time each day should we remind you to review?” after your first review round, when you first turn on a timetable’s Before Class or After Class reminder, or when you start a timer for the first time. All of these are scheduled on your device, and the app has no push server of its own: review reminders contain no card content; a class reminder gives the class name, its time and place, and an after-class reminder how many of its cards are due; timer alerts give only what the timer counts toward or the name of its section, and its length.
  • App icon badge: when “Show Due Count on App Icon” is on, the icon shows how many cards are due today.
  • Microphone: requested the first time you use Say it; it is on only while you Say it, and off as soon as you finish.
  • Speech recognition: requested the first time you use Say it where the system’s speech recognition is needed, to turn what you say into text on your device.
  • Calendars: requested only when you tap “Connect Calendar & Reminders” in the Schedule widget. Schedule reads and writes your calendars directly: it shows your events, and the events you add, change or delete here are saved in your calendars, which send their alerts; the app keeps no copy and uploads nothing, and only remembers on this device which calendars you chose to hide.
  • Reminders: requested together with Calendars when you tap “Connect Calendar & Reminders”, or when you tap Connect by the to-dos in Schedule. To-dos live in your reminders: Schedule shows them, and the to-dos you add, change, tick off or delete here are saved in Reminders, which sends their alerts; the app keeps no copy and uploads nothing, and only remembers on this device which lists you chose to hide.
  • The app does not use location, contacts or health data, and does not read other files on your device except what you hand it yourself: files you choose in the file picker (a PDF, a Word or PowerPoint file, a web page, an Anki deck, a share file or a backup file), what you send to it from another app with Share or Open in Memo Clock, and the folder you choose for Auto Backup.

5. What the app does not do

  • It has no accounts and collects no identity information such as your name, phone number or email address, except in feedback emails you choose to send (see section 9).
  • It contains no analytics, advertising or tracking tools and does not track you across apps.
  • Apart from syncing and storage through your own iCloud and the files you export or share yourself, it does not send your content or the recognized text anywhere; downloading the handwritten-formula reader only fetches files and uploads nothing; for a web page you imported connecting by itself when you open it, see section 3.
  • It connects to no AI service; the handwritten-formula reader is downloaded to your device and runs on it.
  • It has no in-app purchases or subscriptions. Payment and refunds are handled by Apple through the App Store; the developer never receives your payment details.

6. Deleting your data

  • The cards, decks, notebooks and pages, logbooks and pages, cells, notes, word books and words, focus categories and classes you delete in the app go into Recently Deleted (under ··· at the top of the Desk) for 30 days: until then you can put them back, or tap Delete Now; after 30 days they are deleted. Deleting a card also deletes its review history and images; deleting a deck deletes its cards and their review history; deleting a notebook deletes all its pages; deleting a logbook deletes all its pages, cells and photos and the cards made from it. Deletions and Recently Deleted sync to your other devices signed in to the same Apple ID.
  • Deleting a widget deletes what it holds at once.
  • Deleting a word book you made deletes its list of words; what you learned in it and your answers in it are not deleted with it. A word book that comes with the app is only taken off the Desk, and what you learned in it stays.
  • The handwritten-formula reader: delete it in Settings › New Card › Handwritten Formulas.
  • Backup files: a backup you exported is deleted by you, wherever you kept it; the files Auto Backup writes are in iCloud Drive › Memo Clock in the Files app (or the folder you chose), and can be deleted there.
  • Deleting the app deletes what it stored on that device. Data synced to iCloud, the study data in iCloud and the automatic backups in iCloud Drive are not deleted with it; to delete them, open Settings on your device, tap your name, go to iCloud storage management, find Memo Clock and delete its data; the automatic backups can also be deleted in the Files app.
  • Data in device backups is managed by your backup settings.

7. Children

The app collects no one’s identity information, including children’s.

8. Changes to this policy

If a future version changes how data is handled, we will update this policy first and update the date both in the app and on the web page.

9. Feedback and contact

Settings › About › Contact Support opens a message to the developer, addressed to 3528872473@qq.com, in your device’s mail app. The message comes with the app version, device model, system version and interface language filled in, and you can change or delete any of it before sending. The developer receives the message only if you send it yourself; it then includes your email address, what you wrote and anything you attached. This is used only to reply to you and to improve the app, never for anything else, and is not shared with any third party. When the device has no app that can send email, the address is shown so you can copy it and write yourself.

Questions about this policy or how data is handled can also be sent to 3528872473@qq.com.
